*Developed 2026-11-14. Basis: Will Bickford's direction of 2026-11-14 — "develop requirements for a hosted service that allows researchers to submit pre-prints for assessment. the researchers own the phext that is produced and can choose on a per paper basis if it joins the commons."*

In what follows, "Assay" and "the service" are the same thing.*

The division of names: **Assay** is the service, fronted at **tluf.org**; **TLUF** also names the frame the service serves — the four bins held as a commons standard, not a product name. *The service is MOAT's process offered as a product. MOAT is the house's research tracker: papers are heated until their individual claims can be pulled out — spaghettified — and each claim is placed into exactly one of four standings, True / False / Unknown / Fiction, on a stated basis. A researcher's standing grows along those four dimensions for the novelty they introduce to the Oracle. This document states what a hosted version of that process must do, for researchers the current system cannot serve: the fringe, whom academia's accept/reject verdict structurally misses.*

## How to read this document

- Requirements are numbered by section: **S** submission, **A** assessment, **P** the produced phext, **O** ownership, **C** the commons, **M** merge and attestation, **D** discovery, **F** forgery checks, **R** researcher scores, **T** operations and trust, **API** API access, billing, and the review queue, **NG** non-goals.
- **MUST** is a hard requirement: the service does not launch, or the feature does not ship, without it. **SHOULD** is required unless a stated reason stands against it in a given build.
- A requirement is written to be testable: each one names a behaviour an inspector can check against the running service or its records.
- Where a genuine decision belongs to Will, the text says **OPEN DECISION (OD-n)** and gives the options and the trade-off. Open decisions are collected again in §9. Nothing in this document decides one silently.
- Vocabulary is Will's where he has supplied it: *commons*, *fringe*, *shape*, *novelty*, *spaghettify*. A paper's **shape** is the correct structure its author found — the problem carved the right way — independent of whether its detailed claims hold. A **nuance failure** is a claim that fails in detail while the shape stands.

## 0. Standing commitments (the ground every requirement stands on)

- **0.1 No verdict is the product.** The service never returns accept or reject. It returns a reading: claims binned per claim, the shape extracted, the nuance failures named. (Will, 2026-11-14: academia "can only reason in truth" and so throws the correct shape away with the failed nuance; the Oracle prices each claim separately and keeps the shape.)
- **0.2 No pedigree anywhere.** No workflow, form, queue, price, or score may require or reward affiliation, institutional email, publication history, or credentials. Test: an inspector submits as an unaffiliated author and as an affiliated one and compares every step of handling.
- **0.3 All perspectives are input.** The four bins exist so no submission is turned away for being partly wrong, partly speculative, or partly story. If a class of submission cannot be binned at all, a requirement is breached.
- **0.4 The researcher owns the product.** The phext an assessment produces belongs to the researcher (full treatment in §4). The service is a reader for hire, never a rights holder.
- **0.5 The commons is chosen, per paper.** Nothing joins the commons by default, by bundle, or by account setting that papers inherit silently (§5).
- **0.6 Honesty about the instrument.** The Oracle's own standing is public and current: as of this writing the binning-reliability measure stands at ~62% (55–70) — True gate met, Unknown gate met, Fiction gate half-met (entered by hand only, one source tested), False gate unmet (no grounded False filing has yet survived in the tracker's diet). The service inherits that standing on day one and must say so (T-9); it may not present its binnings as more reliable than the gates support.

## 1. Submission (S)

- **S-1 (MUST)** The service accepts a preprint in at least these forms: PDF; LaTeX source (single file or archive); plain text / Markdown; an arXiv identifier; a viXra identifier; a native `.phext` or `.phextura` file. An identifier submission is fetched by the service; the fetched copy and its fetch date are recorded in the submission record, so the assessment is pinned to the exact text read.
- **S-2 (MUST)** Submission metadata is limited to: title; author list as the submitter states it (names or stable pseudonyms); field tags chosen from the service's tag list plus free tags; contact route for delivery and dispute notices; the per-paper commons choice (default: private — see C-1); a declaration of which authors the submitter speaks for (used by C-3). No field may ask for affiliation, institution, degree, or prior publications, and no optional field may be added that smuggles one in.
- **S-3 (MUST)** No account may require anything beyond a working contact route and a credential the researcher controls. Pseudonymous submission is permitted (but see OD-6 on identity and score accrual).
- **S-4 (MUST)** Every submission carries a visible state, queryable by the submitter at any time: **received → queued → in assessment → assessed → returned**. "Returned" is terminal for that submission; later re-examination is a new event on the same submission (A-11), never a state regression performed silently.
- **S-5 (MUST)** The submitter may withdraw a submission at any point before the state **assessed**. Withdrawal stops work, and the submitted text and any partial work product are deleted under O-5's semantics. After **assessed**, the produced phext is the researcher's to delete (O-5); the service-side submission record reduces to the audit stub (T-4).
- **S-6 (MUST)** Duplicate handling: if the same text (by content hash) is submitted twice by the same researcher, the second submission is linked to the first rather than re-assessed, and the submitter is told. If the same text arrives under different authorship, both submitters are notified that a duplicate exists; the service does not adjudicate authorship (plagiarism is a dispute between researchers, A-12) and does not bin the paper differently for either. A duplicate under different authorship is also a forgery signal and is handed to the checks of §5C (F-2) — detection here, findings there.
- **S-7 (MUST)** Near-duplicates (revised drafts of a paper already assessed) are accepted as new submissions and linked to the prior assessment; the new assessment states its delta against the prior edition where the text materially differs. A researcher's revisions are a normal use of the service, not an abuse pattern.
- **S-8 (SHOULD)** Submission is free of charge at the point of use for at least a baseline tier. If any tier is paid, price may buy depth or queue position only as OD-5 decides — never a different binning standard (A-13).
- **S-9 (MUST)** A submission that cannot be read (corrupt file, unfetchable identifier, unsupported encoding) is returned to the **received** state with the specific failure named and a route to fix it. Unreadable is never a silent rejection.
- **S-10 (MUST)** The submission record states, from the moment of receipt, the current expected turnaround honestly — the live queue estimate of T-8, not a marketing figure.
- **S-11 (MUST)** Every submission passes the forgery checks of §5C at intake (F-3). A check that finds nothing records exactly that; a check that finds grounds produces a finding under F-4's discipline — never a silent flag, a shadow score, or a quiet change in handling.

## 2. Assessment (A)

What an assessment *is*, mechanically: the paper is heated — read in full — until its individual claims can be pulled out; the claims are spaghettified into the four standings; the shape is extracted; the nuance failures are named at the exact line; the novelty is priced per author. The reading is the product. Everything below is checkable against a delivered assessment phext.

- **A-1 (MUST)** Every load-bearing claim of the paper receives exactly one standing — **True, False, Unknown, or Fiction** — each with its basis stated in the assessment: for True, stipulation or a verification receipt; for Unknown, attributed / open / synthesis / estimate, distinguished; for Fiction, construction (the claim is the author's device, not their assertion). A claim with no standing, or two, is a defective assessment.
- **A-2 (MUST)** A claim may be binned **False** only on stated grounds of one of three kinds: (i) a counterexample, exhibited; (ii) an internal break — the claim contradicts the paper's own other claims or its own definitions; (iii) a collision with a held True — a claim standing True in the commons or in the house's record, named by its address. A False with no grounds of one of these kinds may not be issued; suspicion, style, and venue are not grounds. This is the False gate's standard, and it binds the service exactly as it binds the house tracker.

*Amended 2026-11-14 (later the same day) — False filings carry a failure kind. Will, 2026-11-14: "we'll track both malicious and naive failures." A claim binned False under A-2 is recorded with a **failure kind**: **malicious** — the claim fails and intent to deceive is part of the record — or **naive** — an honest failure (a wrong shape, a broken step, an over-claimed nuance). The kind is tracked alongside the filing: it is never a fifth standing, it never changes the bin, and it is never required to bin False — a False issues on A-2 grounds alone, whatever its kind. A malicious kind is recorded only where the stated grounds carry intent; in every other case the kind is naive. This is how TLUF's second word earns its place without an intent standard blocking the False gate: lies are the malicious subset of False, named as such when the grounds carry intent. This note adds the kind to False filings; it changes no requirement above.*
- **A-3 (MUST)** Every assessment contains a **shape extraction**: the correct shape the paper found, stated in the assessment's own words, and — where it rhymes with concepts the commons already holds — linked to those concepts by address. The shape is extracted independently of how the claims bin: a paper whose every detailed claim fails can still carry a shape that stands.
- **A-4 (MUST)** Where no correct shape is found, the shape extraction says exactly that, and says what the paper was reaching for. "No shape found" is a legitimate finding, stated plainly; an assessment that flatters every paper with a shape is as defective as one that finds none.
- **A-5 (MUST)** Every nuance failure is named at the exact line: the claim as written, the standing it received, and the specific point of failure (the step, the case, the quantifier, the collision). A general remark about rigour is not a naming.
- **A-6 (MUST)** The assessment records the **questions the paper opens** — points where the reading could not settle a claim and the claim's standing is Unknown on the open basis, each stated as a question a next paper could close.
- **A-7 (MUST)** Novelty increments are computed per author for the paper (§6) and recorded in the assessment: what is new to the Oracle along each of the four dimensions, priced against the commons' state at the time of assessment (R-4).
- **A-8 (MUST)** Depth is one standard. An assessment either meets A-1 through A-7 in full or is not delivered. The service may offer a explicitly shallower *preliminary reading* only if it is labelled as one in the produced artifact itself and never counts toward scores (R-6); see OD-5.
- **A-9 (MUST)** Turnaround is published per submission as an estimate range at receipt (S-10) and updated when the queue moves. The cost driver is stated to researchers in plain terms: assessment cost is reading cost, and reading cost scales with the paper's length and the density of its claims — not with its author's standing.
- **A-10 (MUST)** Each assessment is signed in its artifact with the assessor process name and version and the date (P-6). An assessment produced under a later process version is a new edition (P-7), never an edit of the old one.
- **A-11 (MUST) Contestability.** A researcher may dispute any binning, shape finding, or novelty price in their assessment, giving their reason. A dispute triggers re-examination of the disputed points by the process, and the outcome — standing confirmed, standing changed, grounds sharpened — is recorded as a **revision** appended to the assessment's record (a new edition of the produced phext), with the dispute and its resolution visible in the artifact's revision history. Silent edits to an issued assessment are prohibited; there is no mechanism in the system that can perform one.
- **A-12 (MUST)** A False binning that is disputed receives re-examination as of right, and the False stands in the researcher's public record (if any) only as "disputed" while re-examination is open. The house standard applies unchanged: a False is fully standing when it survives re-examination.
- **A-13 (MUST)** The binning standard is invariant across submitters, tiers, and the paper's venue of origin. A viXra preprint and an arXiv preprint of the same text receive the same assessment. Venue may be recorded as provenance (P-5); it may not appear as a factor in any standing, ground, or price.

## 3. The produced phext (P)

The assessment is delivered as a phext file — the artifact, not a report about an artifact. The researcher receives the whole thing, in the native format, readable with open tooling.

- **P-1 (MUST)** Each assessed paper produces exactly one phext (per edition). Its contents, at minimum: (a) the claim set — each claim as its own scroll, with its standing, its basis, and its grounds where the standing requires them (A-2, A-5); (b) the shape extraction (A-3/A-4) as its own scroll; (c) the nuance list — every named failure at its line (A-5); (d) the questions opened (A-6); (e) the novelty line — per-author increments along the four dimensions (A-7); (f) the revision history — every dispute and re-examination outcome (A-11), appended, never rewritten.
- **P-2 (MUST)** Claims, shape, and questions are addressed in the phext's own coordinates, so any single claim can be cited, linked, and re-priced individually without moving the rest. Where the phextura design's second address layer fits (global addresses naming the research object — work identifier, edition pin, claim key), the artifact carries those addresses too, so a claim remains findable after the file moves.
- **P-3 (MUST)** The artifact is self-contained: the full assessment text travels in the file. No scroll's meaning depends on a service-side database, a login, or a network fetch. Links into the commons (A-3) are addresses — they enrich the file when the commons is at hand and degrade to plain citations when it is not.
- **P-4 (MUST)** The artifact is readable and writable with open tooling: the reference phext library (libphext) and SQ. No proprietary reader, no encrypted container, no licence check on opening one's own file. Test: an inspector with only the open tools opens the artifact, reads every scroll, and re-serves it locally.
- **P-5 (MUST)** Provenance is carried inside the artifact: submission identifier; the paper's own identifiers as submitted (arXiv/viXra id or content hash of the submitted text); submission and assessment dates; the commons choice in force for the paper; and the assessor signature of A-10.
- **P-6 (MUST)** The assessor signature names the process and its version precisely enough that two editions produced by different versions are distinguishable at a glance, and a researcher can cite which process read their paper.
- **P-7 (MUST)** Versioning is by edition. Any change to an assessment — re-examination outcome (A-11), re-pricing when the commons moves (C-4), a corrected error — produces a **new edition** of the phext with the change recorded in its revision history. Prior editions are preserved and remain exportable by the researcher for as long as they keep the paper on the service. The service never edits an issued edition in place.
- **P-8 (MUST)** The artifact's text is the assessment, whole. Export (O-3) delivers this file itself. A PDF or web rendering may be offered as a convenience view; it is a view, never the deliverable of record, and it must say so on its face.
- **P-9 (SHOULD)** The artifact separates the paper's own material from the assessment's: claims quote or tightly paraphrase the submitted text and are marked as the paper's; shape, grounds, and synthesis are marked as the Oracle's. A reader must never have to guess whose words a scroll carries — the house's synthesis-labelling discipline, carried into the product.

## 4. Ownership (O)

Will's sentence is this section's charter: *the researchers own the phext that is produced.*

- **O-1 (MUST)** The produced phext — every edition of it — is the property of the paper's authors, jointly as their own arrangements provide. The service acquires no ownership interest in it by assessing, hosting, or delivering it.
- **O-2 (MUST)** The service takes no licence over a produced phext beyond what is strictly needed to: store it for the researcher, deliver it to them, and — only for a paper the researcher has joined to the commons — serve the joined material as §5 describes. No other use is licensed by submission, by assessment, or by the terms of service, and the terms must say this in plain words.
- **O-3 (MUST)** Full export at any time: the researcher can download the complete produced phext — current edition and all preserved prior editions — in the native format, without fee, delay, or reduced fidelity. Export is the artifact itself (P-8), never a rendering, a summary, or a partial dump.
- **O-4 (MUST)** Assessment confers no rights over the underlying preprint. The service claims no interest in the paper itself — not in its text, its results, or its future publication — and assessing a paper creates no encumbrance on the author's freedom to publish, post, or submit it anywhere.
- **O-5 (MUST)** Deletion on request: a researcher may delete a produced phext (and, under S-5, a submission) at any time. For a paper that never joined the commons, deletion is full erasure of the artifact and the submitted text from service storage, completed within a stated period and confirmed to the researcher; what remains is only the audit stub of T-4 (identifiers, dates, state history — no content, no claims). For a paper that *has* joined the commons, deletion semantics are OD-1's to settle; until OD-1 is decided, the service must not accept commons joins it cannot honourably unwind under either candidate semantics, and must tell joining researchers which semantics is currently in force.
- **O-6 (MUST) No mining of private phexts.** A non-commons phext is never read by any process other than the assessment its researcher ordered and the storage that holds it: never for model improvement, never for training, never for the commons' benefit, never for another researcher's assessment, never for aggregate statistics that leak its content. This is a hard requirement, not a policy preference; the system's architecture must make the private store unreachable from commons machinery (T-2), so the promise is kept by construction, not by intention.
- **O-7 (MUST)** A researcher's phexts are never shown, quoted, or summarised to any other researcher, including in another assessment's links. A private paper's claims do not exist, for every other user of the service, until its authors join it to the commons.
- **O-8 (SHOULD)** The terms of service are short, plain, and state O-1 through O-7 without legal fog. Where the terms and this section differ, the researcher's reading of this section is the one the service honours in disputes about its own conduct.

## 5. The commons (C)

The commons is the shared Oracle corpus: the assessments whose researchers have chosen to join them. Joining is the service's central social act, and it is a choice, made per paper, by the paper's owners.

- **C-1 (MUST)** The default state of every paper is **private**. Joining the commons is an affirmative act per paper — the **merge** of §5A — taken after the assessment is delivered (a researcher joins a reading they have seen, not one they are promised). No account-level setting may pre-join future papers; a standing preference may exist only as a reminder, never as the act itself.
- **C-2 (MUST)** Joining changes the paper's assessment phext only in its commons status and addresses: the claims, shape, questions, and novelty line already in the artifact are the same claims the commons receives. There is no second, public version edited for appearance.
- **C-3 (MUST)** No paper merges into the commons except through the authentication and attestation procedure of §5A (M-1 through M-8): every author of record authenticates and attests for the exact edition being merged. The remaining multi-author questions — who counts as an author of record for merge purposes, and the hard cases of unresponsive, deceased, or later-disputing authors — are OPEN DECISIONS OD-2, OD-8, OD-9, and OD-10, laid out in §9; §5A does not restate them. Until they are decided, the strictest reading holds: no merge proceeds on a partial set of attestations, and any listed author may block a merge of their paper at any time. A merge performed without the full attestation set is void from the start and is unwound under OD-1's semantics with the error recorded.
- **C-4 (MUST)** What joining does, exactly: (a) the assessment's claims enter the shared corpus, where commons machinery may link to them, price new submissions against them, and re-price them when linked claims move — each re-pricing a new edition under P-7, visible to the paper's owners; (b) the paper's shape becomes findable by other researchers reading in the commons; (c) the authors' novelty from the paper accrues in public (R-3); (d) the paper's questions join the commons' open list, where another researcher's paper may close them.
- **C-5 (MUST)** What joining does not do: it does not transfer ownership (O-1 is untouched); it does not publish the underlying preprint — the text of the paper itself joins only if the researcher separately deposits it, which is a distinct act the service must not bundle into the join; it does not join, expose, or affect any other paper by the same authors; it does not license any use beyond the commons functions listed in C-4.
- **C-6 (MUST)** A joined paper's owners are notified of material commons events touching it: a re-pricing of one of its claims, a dispute opened against one of its standings by another researcher (whose own remedy runs through A-11 on their side), and any other paper's claim filed as a collision (A-2(iii)) against one of theirs.
- **C-7 (MUST)** Withdrawal from the commons must be possible at any time, by the same authority that joined. Its semantics are the honest tension of this design, and they are **OPEN DECISION OD-1**: the house tracker's discipline is no-pruning — a retired item is relocated with a redirect, because the record of what was known, and what other work already leans on it, is itself knowledge; against that stands an owner's right to remove their property from a served corpus. OD-1 lays out both semantics and what each does to inbound links; §9 states them in full. Whichever semantics Will chooses, withdrawal always stops all *new* commons use of the paper immediately, and the withdrawal itself is recorded.
- **C-8 (MUST)** The commons never contains a paper whose owners did not join it. There is no mechanism — administrative, legal, or "for the good of the corpus" — that joins a paper from the service side. Test: an inspector with operator access attempts to find any path that joins a paper without a recorded owner act and finds none.
- **C-9 (SHOULD)** The commons is readable as a corpus: a researcher in the commons can browse joined claims by field and shape, follow links between papers, and see the open-questions list. Read access scope (world-readable vs commons-members-only) is OD-7.

## 5A. Merge and attestation (M)

*Will, 2026-11-14: "Researchers will authenticate and attest before a paper is merged."*

Submission, assessment, and merge are three different acts, and this group keeps them separate. A paper may be submitted and never assessed (withdrawn, S-5); assessed and held private indefinitely (C-1); merged only by the procedure below.

- **M-1 (MUST)** **Merge** is defined as the act by which one edition of a paper's assessment phext joins the commons. Merge is affirmative, per paper, and per edition; it is never a side effect of submission, assessment, account settings, or the passage of time.
- **M-2 (MUST) Authentication.** Before a merge, **every author of record** must authenticate: prove control of the identity under which they are named as an author of that paper. Identity merely asserted at submission (S-2) is not sufficient at merge time. The mechanism is the implementation's choice; the requirement is that control is proven at merge, for each author separately.
- **M-3 (MUST) Attestation.** Each author, as part of the same act, attests in a recorded statement that: (a) the work is theirs to offer; (b) they choose, for this paper, to join the commons; (c) they understand what joining publishes — the assessment's claims, the shape, and their novelty increments (C-4) — and what it does not publish: the preprint itself, unless separately deposited (C-5).
- **M-4 (MUST)** An attestation is bound to **one paper and one edition, by that edition's checksum**. A materially revised edition of the assessment (P-7) requires a fresh round of authentication and attestation before it merges; an attestation for an earlier edition carries no force for a later one.
- **M-5 (MUST)** Attestations are preserved in the commons record as the consent evidence for the merge: who attested, when, and for which checksum. The attestation set of a merged paper is inspectable by its authors at any time, and the fact and date of the merge are recorded in the paper's audit log (T-4) and in the artifact's provenance from the merged edition onward.
- **M-6 (MUST)** By default, **no merge proceeds on a partial set of attestations**. Every author of record must authenticate and attest, or the merge does not happen. Departures from the full-set rule for the hard cases — an unresponsive co-author, a deceased or incapacitated author, a pseudonymous author whose identity is a key they control — are not implementable until OD-8 and OD-9 are decided; M-2's "control of the identity" is deliberately phrased so a pseudonym's key counts as an identity for authentication purposes.
- **M-7 (MUST)** Authentication and attestation must not become a pedigree gate in disguise: the procedure may not require affiliation, institutional email, real-name identity documents, or third-party vouching. Proving *control of the named identity* is the whole requirement; a stable pseudonym with a key is an identity.
- **M-8 (MUST)** An author who attests and later disputes the merge is handled by OD-10's decision; until it is decided, a post-merge dispute by an attesting author is recorded on the paper's commons record immediately, the paper is flagged as disputed, and withdrawal under C-7 remains available to that author on the same terms as any owner withdrawal.
- **M-9 (MUST)** The forgery checks of §5C run again at merge, over the attestation act itself: the identity control proven under M-2, the checksum binding of M-4, and the authorship the attestations claim (F-3). A finding holds the merge under F-5; an attestation whose bytes do not match the edition being merged (F-2) fails by construction, before any question of intent arises.

## 5B. Discovery (D)

*Will, 2026-11-14: "users can find each other when they share a domain or a paper."*

Discovery is the commons' structure made social. A researcher's merged papers place them — in domains, and next to other papers — and the overlaps are the introductions. Nobody is findable here by being listed, ranked, or searched as a person; they are findable because their work and someone else's work touch.

- **D-1 (MUST)** A researcher becomes findable through a **shared domain**: a field or cluster of the commons (the tag space of S-2 as priced against the corpus, and the concept clusters the corpus itself holds) that both researchers' *merged* papers touch. The match is computed from merged material only, and the domain that makes the match is named in it.
- **D-2 (MUST)** A researcher becomes findable through a **shared paper**, in either of two forms the design already supports: (a) co-authorship of record — the same paper stands in both researchers' records; (b) a stated relation between their papers in the commons — one paper's assessment re-priced a claim of the other's (C-4(a)), filed a collision against one of its claims (A-2(iii)), extracted a shape the other's assessment links to (A-3), or closed a question the other's paper opened (C-4(d)). Any further relation kind (citation without re-pricing, shared questions neither paper closed, shared nuance lines) is an extension of this list, added only as a stated amendment to this requirement — never by an implementation quietly widening what "shared" means.
- **D-3 (MUST)** Findability flows from **merged material only**. A private paper contributes no domain, no relation, and no presence to any other researcher's discovery; a researcher with no merged papers is not findable at all. Joining is the act that places a researcher, and it remains per paper (C-1) — a researcher is findable exactly to the extent of what they have chosen to join, and no further.
- **D-4 (MUST)** Findability is the researcher's own setting. The default: a researcher is findable through their merged papers. A researcher may close findability entirely at any time, effective immediately and retroactively — their existing matches stop being shown — without withdrawing any paper from the commons; closing findability is a visibility act, not an ownership act. Reopening is the same researcher's act.
- **D-5 (MUST)** Every match is shown with its reason: the shared domain, named; or the shared paper and the relation that joins the two researchers to it. There is **no naked people-search**: no browsing or searching researchers by name, institution, or field-as-a-person-attribute detached from a shared paper or domain that includes the viewer. Whether a plain name lookup exists at all is a separate, stated choice of Will's (see §9, note N-1); until he makes it, it does not exist.
- **D-6 (MUST)** First contact, where the service offers it, is initiated **through the service**: the message travels under the sender's service identity, and no private channel — contact route (S-2), email, key material — is exposed to either side by the act of matching or of writing. Each researcher controls whether they accept contact through the service at all, separately from findability.
- **D-7 (MUST)** Being findable obliges nothing. There is no reply duty, no read receipt owed, no penalty — in score, in ranking, in any service behaviour — for ignoring a match or a message. Discovery is an offer of introduction; the service manufactures no social debt around it.
- **D-8 (MUST)** Discovery is symmetric and inspectable: a researcher can see, for each of their own matches, the same reason the other side sees, and can enumerate who could find them through each of their merged papers and each domain those papers touch. Nobody is findable in a way they cannot themselves inspect.

## 5C. Forgery checks (F)

*Will, 2026-11-14: "we check for forgeries."*

Attestation (M-2) is the primary defence at merge; the checks in this group are the audit behind it, and behind submission itself. A forgery check is a reading held to the same evidentiary discipline as a False binning — because a forgery finding *is* a False-shaped claim about a person's act, and this house does not issue those on a vibe.

- **F-1 (MUST)** The checks cover three forgery kinds: (i) **identity forgery** — an attestation or merge attempted under an identity the actor does not control; (ii) **document forgery** — a submitted preprint that is another's work presented under new names, or a materially altered copy of a paper already in the commons or in the public record presented as a new work; (iii) **edition tampering** — bytes offered at merge, or in an attestation, that do not match the checksum the attestation was bound to (M-4).
- **F-2 (MUST)** Each kind has its mechanical first test, run before any judgement: (i) identity — the control proof of M-2 itself, re-verified at merge; an attestation whose authentication does not complete is not a finding, it is simply not an attestation; (ii) document — content hashing and text-overlap measurement against the commons corpus and the service's submission record, with any asserted match naming the source it overlaps and the extent of the overlap; (iii) tampering — checksum comparison, which passes or fails exactly. M-9 wires these tests into the merge act.
- **F-3 (MUST)** The checks run **twice**: at submission (over the submitted text and the submitter's claimed authorship; S-11) and again at merge (over the attestation act and the edition; M-9). A pass at submission is not a pass at merge; the merge-time check is the one that guards the commons.
- **F-4 (MUST) The grounds standard.** A forgery **finding** may be issued only on stated grounds of a specific kind: the checksum mismatch itself (bytes named, both hashes shown); a text overlap against a named source, with the overlapping passages exhibited; or a demonstrated identity-control failure (the proof that failed, and how). Resemblance, style, venue, topic, or an assessor's suspicion are not grounds. **"No evidence found" is a legitimate outcome of every check, is recorded as the check's result, and is never an accusation.** A check that cannot state grounds has found nothing, whatever it suspects.
- **F-5 (MUST)** A finding **holds the merge** — that merge, of that paper, does not proceed while the finding stands undisputed or unresolved. A finding does not touch the assessment itself (the reading stands; it is the researcher's property), does not hold the researcher's other papers, and does not appear in any other researcher's view of anything. A hold is a pause with grounds attached, never a mark.
- **F-6 (MUST)** A finding opens a **dispute path** with the same discipline as A-11: the researcher (or an author of record) may dispute the finding with their account of the facts; the check is re-run and the grounds re-examined; the outcome — finding confirmed, finding withdrawn, grounds sharpened — is recorded in the paper's audit log (T-4) and, where the edition already exists, as a revision (P-7). A withdrawn finding is withdrawn in the record, stated as such; it does not linger as an asterisk. There is no silent-flag state anywhere in the system.
- **F-7 (MUST)** Consequences are proportionate and stated in advance. A **confirmed** forgery — a finding that stands after the dispute path is exhausted or undisputed past its stated window — is recorded against the submitting identity in the service's own record: what was forged, the grounds, the outcome. Its effects are limited to the service's own acts: the forged submission or merge carries no score (R-6's voiding discipline), and the identity's future merges receive the merge-time checks with the prior finding visible to the house reviewers of §7A. Whether that record is visible beyond the house — a private flag, or a mark the commons can see — is **OPEN DECISION OD-11** (§9); until it is decided, the record is private to the house and the affected identity, and no public mark exists.
- **F-8 (MUST)** The checks are symmetric with the service's other disciplines: they run identically for affiliated and unaffiliated, pseudonymous and named, first-time and established submitters (A-13's invariance applied to suspicion). Behavioural abuse rates (T-5) may set *how much* checking a flood receives first; they may not set *whose* work is presumed forged.

## 6. Researcher scores (R)

A researcher's score is a **vector of four numbers** — novelty introduced to the Oracle standing True, False, Unknown, and Fiction — accrued only through assessed papers. It is the incentive academia cannot express: novelty is paid in every currency, including a shape that stands while its claims fail.

- **R-1 (MUST)** The vector's components are defined by the assessment record, per author: **True** — new claims and concepts from the researcher's papers that stand True; **False** — their claims filed False on A-2 grounds (a boundary the Oracle now knows; scored as contribution, not as disgrace, and the score display must present it so); **Unknown** — questions their papers opened and claims standing Unknown on the open basis that are new to the record; **Fiction** — constructions standing Fiction that the Oracle took in as devices. Weights and any normalisation are process-versioned with the assessor signature (A-10) and published.
- **R-2 (MUST)** Scores change only through assessed papers. No self-declaration, citation count, social signal, or payment moves any component. Novelty is assessed by the process, never asserted by the researcher.
- **R-3 (MUST)** Visibility: a researcher's vector is private by default. The increments from a paper that joined the commons are public with the paper (C-4(c)); increments from private papers are visible only to the researcher. Whether a researcher may additionally publish their whole vector as one statement is OD-4.
- **R-4 (MUST) Anti-farming, by construction.** Novelty is priced against the commons' (and the house corpus') state at the moment of assessment: a shape the Oracle already holds accrues nothing for being submitted again, by anyone. Consequences the design must exhibit, and an inspector can test: (a) re-submitting a known result under new wording accrues ~zero on every component; (b) splitting one paper's claims across many submissions accrues no more than the single paper would — increments price claims, not submissions; (c) a claim filed False accrues on the False component only when its grounds are new information for the Oracle — re-asserting an already-refuted claim verbatim accrues nothing; (d) colluding submissions that cite each other's novelty into existence accrue nothing, because links between unassessed claims price at zero.
- **R-5 (MUST)** Portability: the researcher's current vector, with the per-paper increments that compose it, is part of their export (O-3) — stated inside a signed phext they own, citable anywhere, verifiable against the editions it sums. A score a researcher cannot carry away is a lock-in device and is prohibited.
- **R-6 (MUST)** Preliminary readings (A-8) and assessments later voided (C-3) contribute nothing to any vector. A re-examination that changes a standing (A-11) adjusts the affected increments in the same edition that records the change — the vector is a sum over current editions, never a fossil of first impressions.
- **R-7 (MUST)** The vector is never collapsed to a single number by the service — not in display, not in any interface, programmatic or otherwise (the API of §7A included), not in any export. Four numbers, always, in the fixed order (True, False, Unknown, Fiction). (See NG-4.)
- **R-8 (SHOULD)** Each component carries its plain-language meaning wherever it is displayed ("boundaries this researcher's refuted claims taught the Oracle", for False), so a fringe researcher reading their own score sees a map of what they gave, not a judgement of what they are.

## 7. Operations and trust (T)

- **T-1 (MUST) Tenant isolation.** One researcher's private material is invisible to every other researcher at every layer: storage, search indexes, caches, logs that carry content, and support tooling. A bug that leaks one private phext across a tenant boundary is a sev-1 incident, disclosed to the affected researcher.
- **T-2 (MUST) Isolation by construction.** Private phexts and the commons live in stores that commons machinery cannot address (O-6). Joining a paper is the only operation that copies material across the boundary, and it copies only the joined artifact, only on the recorded owner act (C-8).
- **T-3 (MUST) Signed assessments.** Every produced phext carries its assessor name and process version (P-5, P-6) and a content hash of the edition. When the process is revised, old editions stand as issued; a researcher may request re-assessment under the new process, which issues a new edition and prices novelty only for what is new against the current corpus (R-4) — history is never quietly re-graded.
- **T-4 (MUST) Audit log.** Every submission has an append-only audit log: receipt, state changes, who/what touched it, assessment events, disputes, joins, withdrawals, exports, deletions — timestamps and actor (researcher, process version, operator) for each. The researcher can read the full log for their own submissions at any time. The audit stub that survives deletion (O-5) is: submission id, content hash, dates, and the state history — no claim text, no assessment content.
- **T-5 (MUST) Abuse handling without a pedigree filter.** An open door to the fringe will draw floods: spam, machine-generated claim-stuffing, grief submissions. Rate handling is permitted only on behaviour — submission volume per contact route, duplicate rates, unreadable rates — never on identity, affiliation, topic, or the assessed quality of prior submissions. Any throttle applied to a researcher is visible to them, states its behavioural ground, and expires. A throttle that a researcher cannot see or appeal is a gate, and gates are what this service exists to replace.
- **T-6 (MUST)** Claim-stuffing is priced, not policed: generated volume accrues no novelty (R-4 prices claims against the corpus, and restated claims are worth zero), so the incentive to flood collapses without anyone being banned for their style.
- **T-7 (MUST) Capacity honesty.** The service publishes its live queue state: submissions waiting, assessments in flight, current expected turnaround range. When the queue exceeds honest capacity, new submissions are told their true wait at receipt (S-10). Depth is never silently traded for speed: the service may close intake temporarily — announced, with the queue state as the stated reason — rather than shallowing assessments to keep the door looking open. The review queue of §7A (API-9) is a queue for the purposes of this requirement: its depth and typical wait are published in the same live state, under the same honesty.
- **T-8 (MUST)** Expected turnaround is computed from the live queue and recent assessment durations and displayed wherever a turnaround is mentioned. A stale or aspirational figure displayed as current is a defect of the same order as a wrong standing.
- **T-9 (MUST) The instrument's standing is published.** The service displays the Oracle's current binning-reliability gates and their standings (today: True met, Unknown met, Fiction half-met, False unmet; ~62%, 55–70) on the submission page and in every produced phext's provenance. When a gate's standing changes on evidence, the display changes the same day. Researchers are entitled to know how reliable the reader is — including, especially, where it is not yet reliable.
- **T-10 (SHOULD)** Incidents that touch assessments — a defective process version, a mis-binning found at scale — are disclosed to every affected researcher with the remedy offered (re-assessment as a new edition, free and by right). The service's corrections are editions and public notes, never silent repairs (A-11's discipline applied to the operator).
- **T-11 (MUST) Feature requests live on the working surfaces.** A researcher can open a feature request from the surfaces they actually work on — the submission view, the assessment view, and their papers list — in at most one obvious step from each. A request channel that exists only on an about page, a footer, or a separate site does not exist for the purposes of this requirement (house discoverability rule: if the user can't find it from the main UI, it doesn't exist).
- **T-12 (MUST) Low ceremony, no standing requirement.** The request form is short: what they want, and why it matters to their work. Any authenticated researcher may submit; no tier, affiliation, publication record, or score standing gates the channel. Requests are weighed on their content, like everything else in this house.
- **T-13 (MUST) Durable records, visible lifecycle.** Every feature request is a durable record with a disposition the requester can follow: **received → under consideration → planned / built / declined**. A decline carries a line of reason, stated in plain words; a request may not sit in *received* forever — an unexamined request older than the service's stated review cadence is a defect. The requester always sees the current state of their own requests. The default posture is a public list of requests and their states, with the requester named where they consented to be named on submission; anonymity on the public list is the requester's choice, never the service's imposition.
- **T-14 (MUST) Built requests are credited.** When a request is built, the service's record credits the requester by name (or their chosen pseudonym) alongside the change — in the release note and in the request's own record, whose state closes as *built*, credited. Contribution to the product is recorded, not absorbed anonymously: the novelty ethic applied to the service itself.
- **T-15 (MUST)** Every forgery check (§5C) is itself auditable: the audit log of the submission (T-4) records that the check ran, at which gate (submission or merge), its outcome — no evidence found, or a finding with its grounds by reference — and every dispute and re-examination under F-6. The grounds and outcomes of a researcher's checks are readable by that researcher in full. A check that leaves no log entry is treated as a check that never ran.

## 7A. API access, billing, and the review queue (API)

*Will, 2026-11-14: "assay has api access as well, billed generously" — "the api creates a queue for us to review."*

The API is a second door into the same house, not a second house. Everything the site does for a researcher's own material, the API does programmatically, under the same requirements; and what arrives through it enters a queue the house reads before it can reach the commons.

- **API-1 (MUST)** The API offers the service's core acts programmatically: submit a preprint (the forms of S-1, as bytes or identifiers), poll a submission's state (S-4), fetch the produced phext (the artifact itself, per P-8 and O-3), list and manage one's own papers, and request a merge. An act available on the site for a researcher's own material and missing from the API is a defect, not a tier boundary.
- **API-2 (MUST)** The API is a channel, never a bypass. Every requirement that binds the site binds the API identically: the metadata limits of S-2 (no affiliation field exists in the API schema either), the ownership rules of §4, the commons rules of §5. **Merge through the API requires the full authenticate-and-attest procedure of §5A for every author of record, exactly as on the site** — an API key authenticates the *client's* identity for submission and management acts; it is not, and cannot be presented as, any author's attestation.
- **API-3 (MUST)** API clients authenticate with **keys bound to a researcher identity** — the same identity that owns the material the key touches. A key can act only on its own identity's submissions and papers; keys are revocable by the researcher at any time, and revocation is effective immediately. Key issuance requires nothing beyond the account requirements of S-3: an API key is not a pedigree either.
- **API-4 (MUST) Billed generously — the posture.** API use is billed, and the billing posture is a requirement, not a pricing table: the free allowance is sized so that **a fringe researcher can do real work — real submissions, real iteration, real retrieval — without paying anything**. Metering exists for volume beyond the generous allowance, not as the door's toll. The concrete allowance, unit prices, and caps are a later pricing decision (**OD-12**, §9); no figure in this document invents them.
- **API-5 (MUST)** Whatever the numbers become, they are **simple, published in advance, and stable within a stated period**: a researcher can compute their bill from the published schedule and their own usage record before the bill arrives. No surge, no opaque "units", no price that depends on who the researcher is (0.2's no-pedigree rule applies to prices: affiliation, venue, and assessed quality never appear in a rate).
- **API-6 (MUST) Ownership acts are never billed.** Exporting one's own produced phext (O-3, any edition), deleting one's own material (O-5), reading one's own assessments, audit logs, and score vector, and withdrawing a paper from the commons (C-7) are free at any volume, through the API exactly as on the site. A researcher never pays to reach, keep, or remove their own property; metering a property right converts it into a rental, and this service does not rent researchers their own work.
- **API-7 (SHOULD)** Pricing is set to invite use rather than to extract rent: where a price could sit anywhere in a defensible range, the requirement is the lower, use-inviting setting, reviewed against the service's actual running cost rather than against what the market for assessment-adjacent services bears. The fringe is the market (0.3); a price that filters the fringe out is a pedigree gate wearing a price tag (T-5's rule, applied to billing).
- **API-8 (MUST) The review queue exists.** Work that arrives through the API enters a **review queue** for the house: submissions taken in, and merges requested, through the API are read by the house — for assessment quality and for the forgery signals of §5C — before a commons merge from an API submission finalizes. A merge requested through the API never finalizes unreviewed, however complete its attestation set (M-6); review is the house's own act, additional to the authors' attestation, not a substitute for it.
- **API-9 (MUST) Queue states are visible to the submitter.** Every queued item carries a state the submitter can poll through the API and see on the site: **queued → under review → cleared / held**. A hold states its reason in plain words (a forgery finding under F-4's grounds, an assessment-quality defect to be repaired by a new edition, an incomplete attestation set) and names the path out of the hold. No item is ever in an unlabelled state.
- **API-10 (MUST) The queue's depth and typical wait are published** — in the live queue state of T-7, disaggregated enough that a researcher can see the review queue's own numbers, not a blend with the assessment queue. A stale or blended figure displayed as the review queue's state is the same defect class as T-8's.
- **API-11 (MUST) Nothing in the queue is silently dropped.** An item leaves the review queue in exactly three ways: it clears; it is held with its stated reason (and stays visible to the submitter as held until resolved or withdrawn); or the submitter withdraws it. There is no timeout-expiry, no overflow shedding, no quiet deletion. An item the house has not reached is *queued*, and says so, for as long as that remains true.
- **API-12 (MUST) Review capacity is stated, and a backed-up queue is announced.** Review is the house's work and its honest constraint; when the queue exceeds the house's reviewing capacity, the service says so on the submission surfaces — site and API responses alike — with the current expected wait (API-10), rather than quietly slowing assessments or merges and letting submitters discover the delay. If review capacity forces a choice, depth of review is not silently traded away (T-7's rule applied to review): intake may pause, announced, before standards thin.

## 8. Non-goals (NG)

- **NG-1** Not a journal. The service issues no acceptance, no rejection, no "quality" grade of a paper as a whole. Any feature that drifts toward a per-paper verdict is out of scope by definition.
- **NG-2** Not a repository of record for preprints. The service holds submitted texts to assess them; the preprint itself becomes public only by its author's own separate act (C-5), and the service does not mint citable publication records for preprints as such.
- **NG-3** Not a peer-review replacement market, a hiring signal, or a tenure input. The score vector describes what a researcher's papers gave the Oracle; the service does not certify researchers, rank departments, or score institutions, and it publishes no league tables.
- **NG-4** Not a single number. No composite score, no percentile, no rank ordering of researchers is produced by any service function (R-7).
- **NG-5** Not a plagiarism court. The service detects duplicates (S-6), links editions (S-7), and records disputes (A-11); it does not adjudicate who wrote what first between researchers.
- **NG-6** Not a gate in anyone else's road. The service's outputs are the researcher's property to use as they wish; the service does not design its artifacts as admission tickets for other institutions, and does not report researchers' activity to any third party.
- **NG-7** Not a directory of persons. Discovery (§5B) introduces researchers through shared work — domains and papers — and the service is not a people index: no roster of researchers searchable as persons, no profiles assembled for browsing detached from merged work, no export of "who works on what" as a list. (The name-lookup question is held open as note N-1 in §9; NG-7 bounds whatever it may become.)

## 9. Open decisions (for Will)

Each of these is genuinely open. The requirement text above stands under any of the options unless the option says otherwise.

- **OD-1 — Commons withdrawal semantics** (from C-7, O-5). *Option A — tombstone relocation* (the tracker discipline): withdrawal removes the paper from commons service and search, and leaves a tombstone at its addresses — the fact that the paper was joined, its dates, and the withdrawal — so links from other assessments resolve to a stated absence instead of dangling; the claim texts leave the served corpus. *Option B — full erasure:* withdrawal deletes the artifact from the commons entirely; inbound links dangle and are marked broken at next re-pricing. Trade-off: A preserves the integrity of every other researcher's assessment that linked to the paper (their grounds remain checkable as *having had* a target) at the cost of a permanent public trace of the paper's former presence; B honours the strongest reading of ownership at the cost of holes in other owners' artifacts. A hybrid (tombstone for link targets, erasure of everything else) is statable and may be the real answer; it is Will's to shape.
- **OD-2 — Join authority for multi-author papers** (from C-3). The mechanics are settled by §5A: every author of record authenticates and attests, bound to the edition's checksum, or there is no merge (M-6). What remains open here is only who counts as an author of record when the submission's author list is contested or includes contributors the submitter cannot bind (acknowledged contributors, group names, a consortium). Trade-off: a wide reading of "author of record" maximises consent and can strand papers behind people who never considered themselves authors; a narrow reading (the named submitting authors only) keeps merge possible and puts the consent risk on the attesting set. The hard cases this decision used to carry are now OD-8, OD-9, and OD-10.
- **OD-3 — Audit retention after deletion** (from O-5, T-4). The stub (ids, hash, dates, state history) is specified; open: how long the stub itself is kept, and whether the content hash of a deleted private paper may be retained indefinitely for duplicate detection (S-6) — retaining it is a fingerprint of a paper the owner asked to erase; not retaining it lets a deleted paper re-enter undetected. Trade-off: erasure purity against abuse memory.
- **OD-4 — Publishing a whole score vector** (from R-3). Increments from commons papers are public with the paper regardless. Open: whether a researcher may elect to publish their full vector (including increments priced from private papers — amounts only, never the papers) as a single signed statement, or whether the vector's totality stays private and only per-paper increments are ever public. Trade-off: a portable reputation is the fringe researcher's prize; it is also the first step toward the league table NG-3 forbids the *service* to build — though a researcher's own choice to publish is a different act from the service ranking them.
- **OD-5 — Depth tiers and turnaround** (from A-8, S-8). *Options:* one depth, honest queue (simplest, purest; the wait can grow long when the fringe arrives at scale); a labelled preliminary reading (claims extracted, standings provisional, no score effect) ahead of the full assessment (keeps the door responsive; risks the preliminary becoming the product in researchers' minds); paid priority that buys queue position but never depth difference (funds the reading; risks a two-class door). The cost driver is fixed — reading is the product — so the decision is about what the service honestly offers while a paper waits.
- **OD-6 — Identity for score accrual** (from S-3, R-2). Submission may be pseudonymous. Open: whether novelty accrues to a pseudonym as a first-class researcher identity (maximally open; a score farmed across many pseudonyms is still bounded by R-4's pricing, since novelty — not submissions — is what accrues), or whether accrual requires a real-name identity while pseudonymous papers may still be assessed without score. Trade-off: the fringe includes people whose institutions would punish the work; it also includes farmers.
- **OD-7 — Commons read access** (from C-9). *Options:* world-readable (the commons as public knowledge infrastructure; maximally useful to the next fringe reader; joined claims become findable by search engines and by the institutions the authors may be hiding from — mitigated by C-1's informed, post-assessment choice); commons-members-only (read access limited to researchers with at least one joined paper; a softer public). Trade-off: the commons' value compounds with readers; so does exposure.

- **OD-8 — The unresponsive co-author** (from M-6). One author of record never authenticates: unreachable, unresponsive, or simply silent. *Options:* (a) merge blocked, full stop — consent is unanimous or absent; papers with a silent co-author stay private forever, however much the others want them in; (b) the submitting authors attest, the service records a notice to the silent author's contact route, and the merge proceeds after a stated objection window — with the silent author able to unwind the merge on later objection under OD-1's semantics; (c) merge proceeds on a stated majority of authors of record, dissent and silence both recorded. Trade-offs: (a) is the only reading under which every merged paper carries complete consent, at the cost of stranding real work behind dead addresses; (b) treats silence after notice as tolerable risk and keeps the unwind as the remedy, at the cost of merged-then-unwound churn in the commons; (c) is the loosest and the hardest to defend to the author who wakes up merged.
- **OD-9 — The deceased or incapacitated author** (from M-6). *Options:* (a) the paper is unmergeable — attestation is personal and cannot be supplied by another; (b) an estate or legal representative may attest in the author's place, with the representation itself recorded; (c) the surviving authors attest and the record states plainly that one author could not, the merge proceeding under that stated fact. Trade-offs: (a) is clean and final, and permanently excludes some of the most valuable historical work the fringe holds; (b) imports legal machinery the service otherwise avoids, and "representative" is exactly where pedigree pressure re-enters; (c) keeps the fact honest in the record and lets the work live, at the cost of a merge whose consent set is permanently incomplete.
- **OD-10 — The author who attests, then disputes the merge** (from M-8; interacts with OD-1). The attestation was given, bound to the checksum, recorded — and the author later says the merge was wrong (mistaken identity use, coercion, changed mind, or a claim they never understood (c)). *Options:* (a) the dispute is treated as an ordinary owner withdrawal (C-7) — the paper leaves the commons under OD-1's semantics, the attestation and dispute both stay on the record; (b) the dispute triggers a review of the attestation's validity first — if the attestation itself was defective (the identity was not really theirs, the checksum attested differs from the edition merged), the merge is void from the start and unwound with the defect recorded; if the attestation was valid and the dispute is a changed mind, it proceeds as (a); (c) attestation is binding for a stated period before withdrawal becomes available. Trade-offs: (a) is simplest and most owner-favouring, and makes every merge provisional in practice; (b) distinguishes fraud from regret, at the cost of the service adjudicating the validity of its own consent records; (c) gives the commons stability and is the only option that tells withdrawing authors "not yet" — the one this service's ethic is most reluctant to say.
- **OD-11 — How public is a confirmed forgery?** (from F-7). A confirmed forgery is recorded against the submitting identity in the service's record; open is who beyond the house and the affected identity can see that record. *Option A — private flag:* the record informs the house's own acts only (review attention on the identity's future merges, F-7); no other researcher ever sees it. *Option B — commons-visible mark:* the record (or a summary mark) is visible in the commons, so future co-authors and readers are warned. Trade-off: A keeps the service out of the reputation business entirely and cannot defame; but a forger burned privately can simply approach the next collaborator — or the next pseudonym (OD-6) — unwarned. B warns the commons at the price of the service publishing what is, in effect, a fraud finding about a named person: a False-shaped claim about a person's act, with the defamation exposure that follows if the grounds are ever wrong — and the grounds standard of F-4, however strict, is operated by fallible readers. A middle form is statable (the mark visible only to a researcher contemplating a merge *with* that identity — co-author-visible, not world-visible); it is Will's to shape. Until decided, F-7's default holds: private.
- **OD-12 — API pricing, concretely** (from API-4). The posture is settled in §7A: generous allowance, no charge for ownership acts (API-6), simple and pre-published rates (API-5), prices set to invite use (API-7). Open are the numbers themselves: the size of the free allowance (in submissions, retrievals, or compute), the unit and price of metering beyond it, any caps, and whether paid volume may buy review-queue position (interacts with OD-5's paid-priority option; note API-8's review is a house act, and selling precedence in it sells the house's own attention, which is a different thing from selling queue position in one's own assessment). Trade-off: the allowance must be large enough that the fringe never meets it in honest work, and bounded enough that machine-generated flood volume (T-5) cannot convert the service's reading capacity into a free resource for spammers — R-4 already makes flooding worthless as score, but reading still costs the house real capacity (T-7).
- **Note N-1 — A name lookup is not yet a decision** (from D-5). The amendment that created §5B specified no naked people-search and left any name search as "a separate stated choice". It is recorded here as an unmade choice rather than a decision with options because the choice has not been posed to Will in this form: whether a researcher may look up another researcher *by name* (as distinct from meeting them through shared work). If posed and answered yes, its shape would need stating (what the lookup shows — only what merged work already publishes, plus findability setting D-4's consent — and whether being name-findable is its own separate setting).
