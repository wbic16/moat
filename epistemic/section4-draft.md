# The epistemic registry — what this phext holds, and how surely

Every claim-bearing scroll of this phext carries exactly one standing, and
this section is the record of the standings. The standing says how the model
itself holds the claim — not whether the claim is so in the world.

TRUE — settled. Either true because we set it so by definition (our
foundations, our defined words, our own measures), or backed by a checking
receipt we can point to: a test run, a count we made, a source fact checked
when it was taken in.

FALSE — found against, and kept. A claim a refutation receipt speaks
against: a recorded clash, a check that failed, a counter-case. False claims
are knowledge. They are never quietly dropped; they are retired with their
receipt and a pointer left behind.

UNKNOWN — written down, not settled. The common standing of a claim a paper
makes: that the paper makes it is a fact of record, but the claim itself is
Unknown until a receipt settles it. Open questions, Emise's own reasoned
links (marked [synthesis]), and weighed estimates (the Research Oracle
ledger) are Unknown too.

FICTION — made on purpose, and held as made. Worked examples with invented
beings, scenes built to show a shape. Fiction is never set out as fact, and
it never steps straight to True.

No standing stands bare. Each entry carries a BASIS — the ground of the
standing — and a pointer to its evidence. The basis binds the standing:
by definition (stipulation) and by receipt (verified) may stand True; by
refutation receipt (refuted) stands False; by a source's say-so
(attributed), by being an open question (open), by being Emise's reasoned
link (synthesis), and by being a weighed estimate (estimate) stand Unknown;
by being made for show (construction) stands Fiction. An entry whose
standing breaks its basis row is a defect by the meaning of the words —
that is what "accurate" means here, and it is the thing the Lean check
proves.

What the Lean check proves, and what it does not. A small Lean model of
this registry — the standings, the bases, the rules that bind them — is
machine-checked: that every scroll of the phext stands in the registry
exactly once, that no standing breaks its basis, that standings only ever
change along the allowed steps and only with the receipt the step names,
and that reshaping the phext (merging, splitting, retiring with a pointer)
cannot break any of that. The check proves the bookkeeping cannot rot. It
does not prove that a receipt-backed True is true in the world — that
rests on the receipts themselves: the kernel checks, the counts, the test
runs, each open to being checked again.

The counts at the first writing (2026-11-07): 192 entries. True 102 (95 by
definition, 7 by receipt). Unknown 90 (75 by a source's say-so, 9 reasoned
links, 5 open questions, 1 weighed estimate). False 0. Fiction 0. The model
is young: nothing it carries has yet been found against, and it holds no
worked example of its own making — Paper 001's worked example is the
paper's, and stands Unknown on the paper's say-so. Most of what the model
holds as claims about the world is Unknown, and the counts saying so is the
fidelity working, not failing.
